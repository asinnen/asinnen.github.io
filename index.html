Featured case study · Issue intelligence

Early-warning and issue detection

Connect front-line observations, support cases, device errors, and engineering context to identify emerging patterns across customers.

The operational problem

Signals of a developing issue can appear in different places: team conversations, customer cases, error logs, and engineering tickets. Reviewing each source separately makes broader patterns harder to recognize quickly.

What I built

A connected set of AI-assisted monitoring workflows that groups symptoms, compares customers and historical baselines, and highlights patterns for support leadership to investigate.

Why it matters

Introduces cross-customer early-warning and customer-level monitoring capabilities that were not previously available in this form, while keeping alert review and escalation decisions with people.

Explore implementation and safeguards +
  • Groups front-line escalation messages by symptoms and affected customers.
  • Correlates case clusters with software versions and engineering-ticket context.
  • Compares customer device error rates against peer and historical patterns.
  • Uses volume, customer spread, and persistence rules to reduce noisy alerts.
  • Runs read-only analysis and presents findings for human validation.

Case study · Support operations

Intelligent triage and case assignment

Turn a complex escalation queue into more actionable priorities and reviewer-supported assignment recommendations.

The operational problem

Case ownership and follow-up require balancing technical expertise, current workload, stalled work, and escalation quality. A manual first pass takes time and can make important exceptions harder to spot.

What I built

Tools that recommend owners based on skills and workload, flag missed escalation or de-escalation opportunities, and prepare standup tracking materials.

Why it matters

Automates recurring preparation and surfaces cases that deserve attention, allowing leads to spend more time evaluating and acting on exceptions.

Explore decision rules and related tools +
  • Assignment recommendations account for skill fit and workload.
  • Configurable cap and hold rules help prevent inappropriate routing.
  • Related checks flag stalled cases, chronic vehicle issues, and escalation or de-escalation candidates.
  • Suggested actions are reviewed by team leads rather than automatically applied to source systems.

Case study · Quality program · 2026 beta

AI-assisted quality assurance

Support more consistent case reviews by drafting evaluations while preserving human ownership of scoring and coaching.

The operational problem

Detailed quality reviews require repeated reading, evidence gathering, and scoring across a structured rubric. The time involved limits how efficiently reviewers can prepare useful coaching feedback.

What I built

An AI-assisted review process that drafts evaluations against a 21-item quality form, paired with human confirmation, sampling, and a private coaching tracker.

Why it matters

Early beta use suggests approximately 50% less reviewer time per evaluation. This is an estimate, not a controlled timing study; the program is planned for launch in 2027.

Explore review controls and rollout status +
  • Draft scoring follows the existing 21-item review form.
  • Reviewers verify evidence and confirm or correct the proposed evaluation.
  • Sampling and private tracking support structured one-on-one coaching.
  • Current status: beta in 2026, with launch planned for 2027.

System design

From signals to action: an automated intelligence workflow

Four connected stages turn frontline observations and system data into actionable insights for support leadership.

Stage 1

Front-line signals

Read escalation channels and group messages by symptoms.

Stage 2

Cross-customer clusters

Combine case data, software versions, and engineering tickets; filter noisy spikes.

Stage 3

Customer patterns

Compare device error rates against peers and historical baselines.

Stage 4

Standup and triage

Generate tracking sheets and owner recommendations for lead review.

The lead reviews outputs and decides what to do. Alert thresholds and other rules can be tuned through configuration.

How I lead and build

Practical AI, with accountability

My approach starts with the team's operational pain points, then uses controlled automation to make decisions easier—not to remove human judgment.

Human in the loop

Systems flag, rank, and draft; people review and decide.

Read-only by default

Source systems are queried without changing customer records.

Signal over noise

Volume, customer spread, baselines, and persistence reduce false alarms.

Configurable controls

Thresholds, weights, and keywords can be tuned without rewriting code.

Fair evaluation

Performance reviews count work actually handled and require reviewer confirmation.

Team adoption
